# Interview Preparation Guide

Facts for every answer come from `01-candidate-profile.md`; 01's Framing policy applies. In
interviews the bar is the same in both modes: say only what the candidate can explain and
defend. Any stretch in the documents (truth report) gets a prepared, honest answer.

## STAR format

**Situation** (context), **Task** (the candidate's responsibility), **Action** (what they did),
**Result** (the outcome). 1-2 minutes, specific, ending with what they learned or would do
differently.

## Story index

Build the index from 01 Stories (and Experience and Projects where Stories is thin). Each story
starts as a stub. Draft S/T/A/R from the 01 section named, show it to the candidate, and keep
it only when they approve it. Never invent a detail to complete a story; ask instead.

The index is the table below; its first row only shows the format. Add a row only for a story the candidate
approved (`/interview` appends rows on request). The full S/T/A/R text goes in 01 Stories, so
facts stay in one file.

| # | Story (01 section) | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [short name] ([01 section]) | [the questions it answers, e.g. "a measurable result; a hard decision"] |

Aim for stories that cover, between them:
- something you shipped or finished from start to end;
- a measurable result (business, research or user impact);
- a hard technical or domain problem;
- a mistake or a wrong assumption, and what you changed;
- conflict or disagreement, and how it ended;
- working with people more senior, or with non-experts;
- a choice between approaches, and why;
- high cost of being wrong (safety, money, health, legal);
- leading or mentoring, only as far as 01 supports it.

## Tough questions

Prepare them with the candidate; never invent their answer.
- "Why did you leave [company]?": honest, forward-looking, nothing negative about the employer.
- "You don't have [skill].": name the nearest real strength (from 01) and how they would learn
  it.
- "Where in 5 years?": ambition that fits the role's growth path.
- "Biggest weakness?": a real one from 01 or from the candidate, with how they manage it.
- "Why this company?": specific projects, values, market position or team; never generic.
- A gap in the CV, a short tenure or a career change: a short, true answer from 01.
- Each stretch listed in the truth report, if the Framing policy is `generous`.

## Questions to ask them

- Role: a typical week; success in 6 months; the team's biggest challenge now.
- Team: size and how work is split; the path from idea to finished work; onboarding.
- Tools and growth: the tools and methods for [area]; room for decisions on design or strategy;
  how the team keeps up with new tools.
- Culture: the culture in a few words; professional development; remote or office set-up; new
  work vs maintenance; leadership style; what people who do well here have in common.

## Call tips

Stories written out beside you; water nearby; smile (it changes the voice); ask when a question
is vague; 5 seconds to think is fine; end with "Is there anything else you'd like to know about
my background?"

## After the application

- Never call to "stand out". Respect a stated timeline; with none, after 2+ weeks a short status
  check is fine, as is a follow-up with genuinely new information.
- Any update (invite, rejection, status): a 2-3 sentence thank-you, as a draft the candidate
  sends.

## Roleplay guidelines

When the candidate asks for practice:
1. Ask which role and company to simulate.
2. Warm-up ("Tell me about yourself"), then role-specific technical or domain questions.
3. One or two behavioural questions on the posting's competencies.
4. End with a tough question or a curveball.
5. After each answer: what worked, what to sharpen, and which story fits best.
