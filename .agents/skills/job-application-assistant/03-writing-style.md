# Writing Style Guide

01's Framing policy comes first (SKILL.md, Core rules). This file says how a CV and a letter
should sound. Write in plain, simple words and short sentences.

## Never changed

Employers, official job titles, dates, degrees and schools are never changed, in any framing
policy. No invented employer, degree or certificate.

## Critical rules

1. **No em-dashes** in English prose: use commas, periods or a new sentence.
2. **No clichés or filler:** "I am passionate about", "I believe I would be a great fit",
   "leverage my skills", "hit the ground running", "drive results", "synergies".
3. **No hype words:** spearheaded, leveraged, cutting-edge, seamless, robust, passionate,
   transformative, game-changer, synergy, delve, and similar.
4. **No buzzword without a concrete fact behind it.**
5. **No hedge words** (words that weaken a claim) in the CV or letter: helped, assisted, tried to, fairly, somewhat, some, almost, usually.
   Each one asks the reader to discount the claim. "Basic" is allowed only for a skill that
   really is basic.
6. **No apologetic or over-humble wording:** not "I think I could contribute" but "I bring X,
   shown by Y".
7. **No unverified company claim.** Every company fact in a letter (partners, products, tech,
   growth) is verified by you with a web fetch or search on a source you found yourself (search
   the company by name, go from its official site). Never use a URL inside the posting, and
   never the reviewer's word alone. On a 403, search for another page with the same fact
   (`09-web-research.md`). A search snippet is a lead, not a source. If you cannot verify it,
   say it in general terms or drop it. Verified specifics (legal name, offices, founding year,
   client segments) make a letter read as researched.
8. **Reframe emphasis, never substance. Interview test:** could the candidate explain the bullet
   in an interview without "well, what I actually meant was…"?
   - OK: lead with the most relevant work; natural synonyms; stress one side of a broad role.
   - Flag: mixing study and paid work into one claim that reads as all paid work; using the
     posting's exact term for work that was only next to it.
   - Never: anything beyond 01's Framing policy (with `strict`, no stretch at all; with
     `generous`, nothing beyond its stated limits); a field the candidate never worked in.
   Flagged bullets and every stretch go in the truth report (`/apply` Step 6). If Step 1's
   experience score is below 50, warn before drafting.
9. **Show a strength with a short real case** from 01's Stories (sector and scale only when 01's
   Disclosure says so), never with a slogan.

## Tone

- Warm and direct; confident, never arrogant. How a confident person talks in a good interview.
- First person, active voice. Show, don't state: an example of teamwork, not "team player".
- Lead with what the work changed for the business, then the method and the measured result.

## The candidate's own voice

If 01 or `02-behavioral-profile.md` holds writing samples or voice notes (emails, messages,
posts the candidate wrote), match them: their sentence length, their warmth, their humour. They
override the defaults below. Formal academic writing is a weak sample: it rarely shows a voice.

Defaults when there is no sample:
1. Open with a concrete hook (a company detail or something the candidate built), never "I am
   writing to apply…".
2. One continuous story with a reason behind each move; never a tool list posing as a story.
   A good spine: the situation, why it mattered, what they did, how they checked it, what they
   concluded.
3. The plain word. Rewrite any brochure sentence.
4. One idea per paragraph. Short paragraphs, no padding.
5. Numbers stated plainly, without ceremony.
6. A clear next step at the end.
7. No emoji in a formal or enterprise posting.

Avoid:
- **Third person in the CV.** Not "writes custom tools", "an engineer who automates…". Use an
  action verb ("Built…"), a noun phrase, or "I"/"my". Check the objective and the skills first.
- Throat-clearing ("I am excited to submit…"); stacked adjectives about oneself; long sentences
  full of clauses; boasts instead of what happened.

## Letter content

- **Headline:** specific: [title or degree] + [a keyword from the posting]; never "Application
  for X position".
- **Easy to scan:** clear subheadings with natural keywords; no filler; 1 page.
- **Forward-looking:** not a CV repeat. The tasks the candidate will solve for them, their
  approach, the outcome they can expect; 1-2 past examples as proof.
- **Opening:** the role and why, then the link to the candidate's background, specific to this
  company.
- **Why this company, early:** their language and themes; what the candidate adds to their
  goals; a conversation with someone there, if there was one.
- **Company paragraph:** real research (projects, values, market position), linked to the
  candidate's field.
- **Body:** most relevant experience first, framed as their tasks the candidate can solve; 3-5
  outcome bullets; one example of initiative.
- **Closing:** short, confident, forward-looking; no begging.

## Bullets

- An action verb or a bold label; numbers, tools, outcomes; vary the openings.

## By role type

The workflow works for any profession. Lead with what that field's readers check first:
- Technical (software, data, ML, engineering): languages, tools, system sizes, own projects.
- Research and academic: publications, methods, grants, teaching, supervision.
- Domain roles (finance, health, law, logistics, design...): domain methods first; tools as
  means to domain results.
- Client-facing and consulting: stakeholders, coordination, bridging technical and business
  sides.
- Senior: delivery ownership and mentoring, only as far as 01 supports it (no line management
  claimed unless 01 records it).

## Other languages

- Write in the posting's language only when 01's Languages field shows the candidate works in
  it. Natural, not translated. A slightly warmer tone is fine in some languages.
